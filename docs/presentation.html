Why MinKNOW plus NanoComp?

MinKNOW explains the sequencing run: device, flow cell, kit, basecalling, and run counters.

NanoComp/NanoPlot explain the analyzed FASTQ: read counts, bases, length, N50, and quality distribution.

The downstream workflow uses FASTQ-level evidence because those are the reads that enter alignment, classification, and assembly.

Filtering decision

Q10 keeps the usable signal; Q15 and Q20 become too expensive

Q10 retains 75.2% of reads and 82.2% of bases. Q20 retains only 8.4% of reads.

Why this host method?

Tool choices were made to match ONT reads and the assignment scale

minimap2 for host alignment

Chosen because map-ont is designed for noisy long reads, runs quickly on large genomes, and produces compact PAF summaries.

Why not BLAST or short-read aligners?

BLAST is too slow for six large genomes at this scale. Short-read aligners are less natural for noisy ONT read length/error profiles.

Decision metric

Use a combination of primary mapped reads, aligned bases, and mapping quality instead of a single count.

Interpretation guardrail

Low host fraction is not a failure. It is exactly what a fecal sample should look like when microbiome DNA dominates.

The assignment did not require a perfect host assembly; it required a reasoned comparative alignment decision. Domestic cat is the strongest supported host under that rule.

Functional host region

BMP6 is the best interpretable host-region candidate, but it needs a caveat

Read-to-GFF overlap finds BMP6 as the top summarized annotated gene.

Biological role

BMP6 is a bone morphogenetic protein family member in TGF-beta signaling, with roles in development, skeletal biology, and iron/hepcidin regulation.

Why not a predicted LOC gene?

BMP6 has a recognizable biological function, making it a better presentation target than a high-count predicted locus with unclear interpretation.

BMP6 locus inspection

The genome-browser view is where we stay honest

Most BMP6-supporting reads form a narrow low-MAPQ pileup; two separate MAPQ-60 reads support a second interval. Good presentation claim: BMP6 is a candidate functional region, not complete high-confidence gene coverage.

Why Flye?

Practical long-read assembler for noisy ONT data. Lighter for this notebook workflow than Canu, and more appropriate than short-read assemblers.

Why dnadiff?

Directly answers the assignment's correctness question with aligned coverage, identity, SNPs, and indels against a public reference.

Assembly result

The assembly is fragmented, but strongly reference-supported

Flye assembly: 3.86 Mbp, 24 contigs, longest contig 577,987 bp, N50 249,377 bp.

dnadiff: 94.17% of assembly aligned, 88.73% of reference aligned, 99.33% average identity.

This is not a closed isolate-grade genome. It is a metagenomic long-read species recovery with strong reference similarity.

Final message

What we learned from Group D's unknown sample

The most defensible biological story is: a domestic-cat fecal sample with a strong Proteus mirabilis signal, plus enough long-read evidence to recover a fragmented but high-identity assembly for the dominant microbe.

Technically

QC and filtering preserved enough signal for host comparison, taxonomy, and assembly.

Bioinformatically

Independent tools converged: minimap2 for host, GFF overlap for BMP6, Kraken2 for microbes, Flye plus dnadiff for assembly.

Biologically

The host and microbiome findings are plausible for fecal material, with caveats stated where evidence is limited.
