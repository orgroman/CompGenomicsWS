Quality control

First question: are the reads usable?

Answer:

Yes. The reads are noisy, as Nanopore reads usually are, but still usable for long-read alignment and microbial screening.

Why these reports? MinKNOW describes the sequencing run itself. NanoComp and NanoPlot summarize the reads after the fact, which is better for reproducible QC in the notebook. FastQC is common for short reads, but less informative for Nanopore length distributions.

First try

Let the reads vote: which genome do they align to?

Domestic cat had the strongest host signal.

It had the most primary mapped reads and the most aligned bases among the candidate genomes.

Why minimap2? It is fast, widely used, and tuned for long noisy reads. BWA-MEM is excellent for many Illumina-style tasks, but minimap2 is the practical default for Nanopore host screening. BLAST would be too slow at this scale.

Microbiome

A fecal sample rarely travels alone

After host screening, the next question was: who else is in there?

For that, we used taxonomic classification rather than host-style whole-genome alignment.

Why Kraken2? Kraken2 is fast and works well when the question is "what taxa are represented here?" Centrifuge is another lightweight classifier, MetaPhlAn is strong for marker-gene profiles, and KrakenUniq adds extra support information. Kraken2 was the best fit for a quick, reproducible species screen.
Plain version:

Host alignment asks "which animal genome?" Kraken2 asks "which microbial names do these reads most resemble?"

Assembly

Can we reconstruct the microbial co-star?

Why assemble Proteus mirabilis?

It had the strongest species-level signal, so it was the best candidate for a focused assembly attempt.

Why Flye? Flye is built for noisy long reads and is commonly used for bacterial Nanopore assemblies. Canu is powerful but heavier, Raven is fast and lightweight, and Shasta is excellent in some long-read contexts. Flye was a balanced choice for this final-project dataset.

Assembly result

The assembly is fragmented, but biologically useful

What this means:

A perfect bacterial assembly would be closer to one complete chromosome. Here we got a partial but coherent assembly from a mixed fecal sample.

Good enough for:

Checking whether the dominant microbe can be reconstructed and compared against a reference.

Validation

Does the assembly look like Proteus mirabilis?

Average identity: 99.33%

The assembled sequence aligns very closely to the reference where it aligns.

Coverage is not complete.

88.73% of the reference and 94.17% of the assembly aligned in the comparison.

Why dnadiff? It gives a compact reference-vs-assembly comparison. QUAST is a strong alternative for broader assembly QC, and minimap2 plus custom summaries would also work, but dnadiff gives the most direct identity summary here.

What we trust

Strong conclusions, careful caveats

We trust these results

  • Host origin is domestic cat based on read count, aligned bases, and MAPQ.
  • Proteus mirabilis is the dominant species-level microbial call.
  • The Proteus assembly is close to a reference over aligned regions.

We are careful about these points

  • BMP6 is a read-overlap observation, not a functional phenotype claim.
  • Kraken2 depends on the database and can misclassify reads from close relatives.
  • The assembly is fragmented and from a mixed sample, so it is not a finished genome.
