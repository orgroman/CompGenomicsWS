Step 3: Functional region that was sequenced

After choosing cat, we looked for a functional region

README instruction:

What we did:

Used the cat genome annotation file and counted which annotated gene regions overlapped our cat-aligned reads.

Step 3 result

BMP6 was our functional-region example

Why BMP6?

It was the top labeled gene region in the overlap table: 35 reads overlapped the BMP6 interval.

What BMP6 is:

BMP6 is a mammalian gene in a signaling family often discussed in development, bone/cartilage biology, and iron regulation.

What we are not saying:

We are not saying this cat had a BMP6 mutation, phenotype, or special biology. We only found that this annotated region was represented in the host reads.

Step 4: Microbiome exploration

Classify the remaining reads

After host screening: who else is in there?

The README says most reads in fecal samples come from microbes living alongside the animal.

Why Kraken2? The README lists Kraken2 and Centrifuge as taxonomy-classification tools. We used Kraken2 because it is fast, works with Nanopore reads, and has small downloadable databases.
Output:

Tables and plots of top genera/species in the microbiome profile.

Step 4 method

Kraken2 uses k-mers for taxonomy classification

1Split readsKraken2 breaks reads into short k-mers.
2Compare to databaseThe database connects k-mers to known organisms.
3Summarize taxaThe result is a microbiome classification table.
Why this fits the task:

The assignment asks for microbiome composition, diversity, and top taxa. Classification is exactly that question.

Why we still keep caveats:

The answer depends on the database. Closely related organisms can share many k-mers.

Step 5: Species assembly

Choose one abundant microbe and assemble its genome

1Pick speciesProteus mirabilis was the top species-level call.
2Assemble readsFlye assembled the selected long reads into contigs.
3Assess correctnessdnadiff compared the assembly to a reference genome.
What Flye did: Flye is the assembler: it reconstructs contigs from noisy long reads. It is not the classifier and not the validation step.
Why Flye? Flye is built for noisy long reads and is commonly used for bacterial Nanopore assemblies. Canu is powerful but heavier, Raven is fast and lightweight, and Shasta is excellent in some long-read contexts. Flye was a balanced choice for this final-project dataset.

Step 5 validation

Compare the assembly to a reference genome

The assembled sequence aligns very closely to the reference where it aligns.

Coverage is not complete.

Why dnadiff? The README suggests dnadiff from MUMmer for comparing the assembly to a reference genome. We used it to report identity and aligned coverage.
